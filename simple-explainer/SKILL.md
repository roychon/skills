# Simple Explainer

## Overview
Explain any concept at the "ball in a bowl" level: one everyday picture carries the idea, one tiny concrete example proves it, and one line sums it up. The reader should get it in under 30 seconds.

## The reply IS these parts, in this order
1. **One-sentence answer.** The idea in everyday words, no jargon or symbols.
2. **The picture.** One everyday scene the reader has lived (a ball in a bowl, a queue at a coffee shop, a library catalogue). Two or three short bullets that map the scene onto the idea, including what goes wrong or what the contrast is.
3. **Tiny example.** The smallest concrete case that shows the idea happening, worked step by step in 3–5 short lines. Use real numbers when the idea is quantitative (round ones: 1, 2, 10, 0.5); otherwise use a specific named case (3 books, one customer, a 5-word sentence). Arrows (`1 → 0.5 → 0.25`) or a 3-row table work well.
4. **The key rule.** The one thing to remember or check, as a plain rule (`|g| < 1 → stable`, `index = lookup is fast, writes get slower`). At most two bullets.
5. **Why it matters.** One or two sentences linking back to what the user asked about or is working on.

Total: about 120–200 words. Bold the key words. Short lines.

## Choosing the picture
- Familiar and physical: motion, money, food, queues, travel, rooms, conversations.
- It must show the failure or contrast case too, not only the happy case.
- One picture, used throughout the reply.

## Choosing the example
- Small enough to do in your head; specific enough to be real (named items, actual values).
- Show the two sides of a contrast side by side.
- If the explanation makes a claim ("it cancels", "it's faster", "it doubles"), the example shows it happening instead of asserting it. For quantitative claims, compute both cases and show the ratio.

## Quick reference
| Part | Length | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Answer | 1 sentence | plain words |
| Picture | 2–3 bullets | everyday scene |
| Example | 3–5 steps | numbers or named case |
| Rule | 1–2 bullets | the thing to remember |
| Why | 1–2 sentences | link to the user's context |

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Opening with the definition, formula, or technical term | Open with the one-sentence answer and the picture; the formal version goes in "The key rule" |
| Asserting a result | Show it in the tiny example |
| Extra sections (history, edge cases, related ideas) | Keep to the five parts; offer the extra in one line at the end |
| Several analogies | Pick one and stick with it |
| Undefined jargon | Say it in words first, then name the term in parentheses |

If the user asks for a derivation, proof, or "step by step", give the full working instead and keep the picture as a one-line opener.
